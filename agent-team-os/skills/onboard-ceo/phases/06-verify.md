# Phase 6 — Verify

**5 minutes.** What is true now, and what happens tomorrow.

## Check these, out loud, together

| # | True? | How they check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A private repo exists in **their own** account | They open it. Not their coach — them |
| 2 | `shared/` has real content in it | Open `shared/ceo.md`, see their own words |
| 3 | A brief exists in `briefs/` about their business | Open it |
| 4 | A commit exists that they did not author | The GitHub commit list |
| 5 | A change they asked for is in the history | Phase 5's commit |
| 6 | A routine is scheduled for Monday 06:00 | Their scheduled tasks list |

If 4 is missing because Run now failed, that is recorded, not hidden — it gets checked
tomorrow morning instead.

## The two questions they should be able to answer

Ask them. Do not answer for them.

1. *"Where does your business information have to live, and why there?"*
   → In the repo. Because Monday's run happens on a machine that never sees their laptop.

2. *"What happens if it doesn't know a number?"*
   → It writes **not measured** and says what would supply it. It never guesses.

If they cannot answer the second one, spend a minute on it. It is the thing that decides
whether they trust the brief in three months.

## What to tell them about tomorrow

> *"Tomorrow at six it runs on its own. You don't need to do anything — don't open your
> laptop, don't check on it. Open `briefs/` when you get up and read the newest file."*

Then, if this was a live session, the promise made in the room:

> *"I'll check tomorrow morning that yours fired, and I'll tell you either way."*

**Make that check.** A promise like that, unkept, costs more than never making it.

## The take-home

Two things, and only two:

1. **The full boundary question.** Phase 3 got one sentence out of them in a room. The rest
   is done alone: what only they decide, what they would never want drafted for them, what
   they want to be asked about even when the answer looks obvious. Fifteen minutes, somewhere
   quiet, this week
2. **Whatever Block 6 asked for.** The first brief's *What I could not see* is a to-do list
   written by the system about itself. One item is enough to make next Monday better

## Do not

- Do not add connectors. Not now, not as homework. That is a separate, supported piece of
  work and self-serve OAuth on a corporate account fails in ways nobody in this session can
  fix
- Do not send them off with a list of six things. Two, or they do none
