# Phase 5 — Change one thing

**8 minutes. Never skip this phase.**

Every tool that has ever died in a CEO's hands was easy to generate and impossible to
maintain. They get a beautiful first output, then something changes in the business, they
cannot change the tool, and by week eight it is confidently producing something stale.

**This phase is the antidote, and it has to happen while somebody is watching.**

## The idea, in one sentence

> **"You never edit anything in here. You ask, and it changes."**

## What to do

Ask them for one real change. Not a hypothetical.

> *"Something in what you told me half an hour ago is already slightly wrong, or you've
> thought of something better. What is it?"*

If they cannot think of one, offer these:

- *"Track gross margin instead of revenue."*
- *"Stop tracking the second metric — it never tells me anything."*
- *"Add Priya to the team, she owns delivery."*
- *"Move my brief to Sunday night."*
- *"Add my second business."* — this one is excellent if they have one, because it is the
  question they have been sitting on since phase 3

Then **they** type it, in their own words, to the Chief of Staff. Not you.

## What the Chief of Staff does

1. Says which file it is about to change and what the change is
2. Makes the change
3. Shows the before and after in one or two lines
4. Commits it with a message a human can read

Then send them to GitHub to look at the commit.

> *"That's the diff. You changed your own system by asking for it in a sentence."*

## Why this is the phase that sells it

Somebody in the room is quietly wondering what happens in three months when the business has
moved on and the thing is still reporting on the old shape of it. **This is the answer, and
they need to see it happen rather than be told it.**

For a coach in the room, it is a bigger moment than the brief itself. A brief proves the
software can write. This proves their client can own it without them.

## Do not

- Do not do it for them. Their hands, their words. If they are stuck on phrasing, tell them
  to say it the way they would say it to a person
- Do not accept a hypothetical. A real change to a real file, committed
- Do not let it run long. One change, eight minutes, move on
